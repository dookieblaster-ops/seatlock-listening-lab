- **Walls vibrate** (Claude, 2026-11-13, owner's go-ahead of that day). Each flat wall of a printed cabinet with one air space is a plate of its wall build's layers, pushed by the pressure inside, and it sends out sound of its own; the page, scores, the optimizer, and the generator hear it. So solid, sand-filled, plaster-filled, and two-filament walls now sound different, not only cost different. On `sealed-test` (8 mm walls), against rigid walls between 100 Hz and 3 kHz: solid PLA rings at 284 Hz (5.5 dB at 291 Hz); plaster-filled is the quietest (resonance 345 Hz, 2.7 dB); a foamed LW-PLA core 6.1 dB at 327 Hz; sand between 1.6 mm skins makes a heavy, floppy wall (resonance 33 Hz, 7.5 dB, and the bass changes); a soft varioShore TPU core damps but softens the wall (137 Hz, 9.4 dB at 197 Hz). Limits, all in the label (as of that day): imported cabinets, MDF and plywood boxes, partitions, and curved skin kept rigid walls (divided cabinets, foam-board boxes, and tube end caps were added the same day; MDF/plywood, braces, partitions, and imported mesh walls followed on 2026-11-15); the walls feel the chamber's mean pressure; the air's load on them, the driver's frame, and holes are left out; PLA's damping and the TPU core's stiffness are not published, so the lab assumes or derives them.
